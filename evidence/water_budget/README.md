# What the budget ratios 0.80-0.90 correspond to (13 Nov 2026)

The water budget of the allocation model is a scenario: rho x the district's baseline irrigation demand.
No measured sustainable yield exists by district. The range is anchored to published evidence and to
current regulation; nothing here enters a calculation. Papers were read as abstracts in the
Semantic Scholar / OpenAlex index (via Consensus); volume, pages and DOI are added at the reference stage.

| Evidence | What it says | Bearing on the budget |
|---|---|---|
| Mojid et al. 2019, Water | 350 wells, 1985-2016: annual maximum depth falling significantly in 65.7% of wells, annual minimum depth in 69.7%; water table below the suction limit for 3-6 months in 59.7% of wells | present abstraction (rho = 1.00) is not sustainable in much of the region |
| Salem et al. 2017, Water Resources Management | NW Bangladesh: keeping the water table within shallow-tubewell suction lift needs a 40% cut in pre-monsoon groundwater-irrigated agriculture, or 10% if over-irrigation is cut and harvest is rescheduled | the needed cut lies between 10% and 40%: rho = 0.60-0.90; the paper's 0.80 and 0.90 are inside this range |
| Ministry of Water Resources gazette of 28-30 Oct 2025 (Bangladesh Water Act 2013, s.17), as reported by Bangladesh Post, bdnews24, Prothom Alo, Dhaka Tribune | 47 unions "extremely high", 40 "high" and 66 "moderate" water-stressed in 25 upazilas of Rajshahi, Chapainawabganj and Naogaon; new tube wells and groundwater extraction for uses other than drinking restricted, for ten years | regulation already aims at cuts far beyond 20% in parts of three study districts |
| Dey et al. 2017, Groundwater for Sustainable Development | water table fell 4-12 m in Rajshahi 1981-2014; Boro area tripled; recommends low-water crops, volumetric charging, AWD | names the same three levers the paper evaluates |
| Mainuddin et al. 2020, Agricultural Water Management; Tulip et al. 2022, Agricultural Water Management | 420 Boro plots: farmers supply close to the requirement; percolation and seepage return to the aquifer; about 33% of irrigation becomes return flow (Rajshahi) | CAUTION for AWD: the model budget is pumped water (net requirement incl. percolation). AWD cuts pumping and its cost, but part of the saving is return flow, so the gain for the aquifer balance is smaller than the gain in the pumping budget |
| Ali et al. 2023, Water Policy; Hossain et al. 2021, Bangladesh Rice J 25(1):37-50 | MODFLOW, Barind: replacing 30-100% of Boro slows the water-table decline; AWD saves 20-25%, pipe distribution about 25% | supports the scenario levers |

Wording for the paper: "A budget ratio of 0.80-0.90 represents a 10-20% cut in pumped irrigation water,
within the 10-40% range that Salem et al. (2017) estimate is needed to keep water tables within suction
lift; it is a planning scenario, not a measured sustainable yield."
